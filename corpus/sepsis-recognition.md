## Recognition

Suspect sepsis in any patient with a suspected infection plus evidence of organ dysfunction. Organ dysfunction can be identified by an increase in the sequential organ failure assessment score of two points or more, or at the bedside by altered mentation, a systolic blood pressure of 100 mmHg or less, and a respiratory rate of 22 breaths per minute or more. A raised lactate above 2 mmol/L in the presence of suspected infection indicates tissue hypoperfusion and identifies a patient at substantially higher risk of deterioration.

## Laboratory signals

A white cell count above 12 x 10*3/uL or below 4 x 10*3/uL, a rising C-reactive protein, a falling platelet count and a rising creatinine together suggest an evolving septic process with organ involvement. No single laboratory value confirms or excludes sepsis, so interpret trends across the panel alongside the clinical picture rather than in isolation.

## Initial management

Take blood cultures before antimicrobials where this does not delay treatment, and administer appropriate broad spectrum antimicrobials within one hour of recognition of sepsis with shock. Give intravenous crystalloid for hypoperfusion and reassess response, measure lactate and repeat it if the initial value is elevated, and escalate to critical care review when hypotension persists after initial fluid resuscitation.

## Source control and de-escalation

Identify and control the source of infection as early as feasible. Review the antimicrobial choice against culture results within forty eight to seventy two hours and narrow the spectrum where the organism and sensitivities allow, because unnecessarily prolonged broad spectrum therapy drives resistance and adverse effects without benefit.
