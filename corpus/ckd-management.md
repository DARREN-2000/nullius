## Staging and progression

Chronic kidney disease is staged by estimated glomerular filtration rate and albuminuria category. An eGFR between 30 and 44 mL/min/1.73m2 corresponds to stage 3b disease. A sustained decline in eGFR of more than 5 mL/min/1.73m2 per year is defined as rapid progression and warrants review of reversible contributors. Confirm any apparent decline with a repeat measurement, because a single result can reflect intercurrent illness, volume depletion, or a recent nephrotoxic exposure rather than true structural loss.

## Monitoring frequency

Patients with stage 3b chronic kidney disease should have eGFR, serum creatinine, potassium and haemoglobin measured at least every three to six months. Urine albumin to creatinine ratio should be measured at least annually because albuminuria independently predicts progression and cardiovascular risk. Increase monitoring frequency after any change to renin-angiotensin system blockade or diuretic therapy.

## Hyperkalaemia in chronic kidney disease

Serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L requires prompt review and a potassium above 6.0 mmol/L is a medical emergency requiring urgent electrocardiography and treatment. Reduced glomerular filtration limits potassium excretion, so patients with chronic kidney disease tolerate additional potassium-retaining exposures poorly. Review renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, and dietary potassium load before assuming an intrinsic tubular defect. Do not routinely discontinue renin-angiotensin system blockade for a mild rise in potassium, because the long-term renal and cardiovascular benefit is substantial; instead correct the contributing exposures and recheck.

## Nephrotoxic medicines

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs reduce renal perfusion and should be avoided in chronic kidney disease wherever an alternative analgesic exists. Review all renally cleared medicines whenever eGFR falls by more than 20 percent from baseline. Metformin requires dose review below an eGFR of 45 mL/min/1.73m2 and should be avoided below 30 mL/min/1.73m2. Iodinated contrast, aminoglycosides and proton pump inhibitors should be assessed for necessity at each review.

## Anaemia of chronic kidney disease

Haemoglobin below 11.0 g/dL in chronic kidney disease should prompt evaluation of iron status, including ferritin and transferrin saturation, before considering erythropoiesis-stimulating agents. Anaemia in chronic kidney disease is driven by reduced erythropoietin production and functional iron deficiency, and a falling haemoglobin alongside a falling eGFR is consistent with progression rather than an unrelated cause.

## Referral to nephrology

Refer to nephrology when eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73m2, when albuminuria exceeds 300 mg/g, when there is rapid progression, or when hyperkalaemia is persistent despite correction of contributing exposures. Refer urgently if there is suspected rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis or an unexplained fall in eGFR of more than 25 percent.
