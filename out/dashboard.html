Medication safety

Curated rule table with stated mechanism and source. An LLM is never asked whether two drugs interact.

major Lisinopril + Spironolactone

Risk of severe hyperkalaemia and arrhythmia.

Mechanism. ACE inhibitor plus potassium-sparing diuretic: additive potassium retention via reduced aldosterone activity, compounded by reduced renal excretion in CKD.

Action. Recheck potassium and renal function; consider dose reduction or withdrawal of one agent. — KDIGO CKD medication safety guidance

major Lisinopril + Ibuprofen

Acute kidney injury and reduced antihypertensive effect.

Mechanism. NSAID inhibits prostaglandin-mediated afferent arteriolar vasodilation while the ACE inhibitor blocks efferent constriction, collapsing glomerular filtration pressure.

Action. Avoid routine NSAID use in CKD; prefer paracetamol or topical analgesia. — NICE CKD guidance on nephrotoxic medicines

major Ibuprofen · Chronic kidney disease stage 3

Accelerated loss of renal function.

Mechanism. NSAIDs reduce renal perfusion in patients already dependent on prostaglandin support.

Action. Deprescribe where possible and document analgesic alternative. — NICE CKD guidance on nephrotoxic medicines

moderate Spironolactone + Ibuprofen

Further elevation of serum potassium.

Mechanism. NSAIDs reduce renal potassium excretion, adding to potassium-sparing diuretic effect.

Action. Monitor potassium closely if combination cannot be avoided. — Local formulary interaction table
